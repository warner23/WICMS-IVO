Thought for 21s

Yes — this is buildable inside WICMS, but only if you treat it as a suite of tightly related plugins/modules, not as one giant plugin that does everything from day one.

My honest recommendation:

Use WICMS as the platform and admin framework.
Do not start over with a brand-new CRM/POS system yet.

WICMS is a good fit if your goals are:

modular plugin architecture

shared users / permissions / sites

admin dashboards

repeatable install structure

faster delivery using an existing CMS-style core

Where WICMS becomes the wrong tool is if you try to force version 1 into:

enterprise-grade real-time POS at huge scale

complex payroll/HR/finance

hardware-heavy KDS integrations across many vendors

deep multi-country legal compliance in one release

So the right answer is:

Phase 1: WICMS Hospitality Suite
Phase 2: WICMS Hospitality Platform
Phase 3: optional external services/microservices for POS, KDS, mobile sync, analytics

Encryption

Do not try to encrypt everything in the database.

Best practice:

encrypt the most sensitive fields

hash passwords

encrypt API keys, tokens, reset tokens, device secrets

optionally encrypt incident notes / sensitive HR notes / medical-contact-style emergency fields

keep operational data queryable where needed

The ICO says UK GDPR requires data protection by design and by default, and stresses embedding privacy and security into the system lifecycle. Their guidance also highlights information security controls rather than “encrypt everything” as a blanket rule.

So for your suite:

encrypt secrets and special sensitive fields

avoid unnecessary personal data

log access

build retention / deletion rules

support exports and subject access handling

English law / compliance angle

For the compliance module, do not market it as “guarantees legal compliance”.

Market it as:

helps businesses manage food safety procedures

For England-first rollout, your baseline should align with:

food safety management procedures / HACCP-style workflows

SFBB-style operational records for smaller catering businesses

allergen handling

health and safety tasking and risk controls

traceable records and review processes

The Food Standards Agency still provides Safer Food, Better Business for caterers as a core food safety management framework for smaller catering businesses, and it explicitly says businesses must review and update procedures.

Food businesses in England also need to register premises at least 28 days before opening, and some businesses handling products of animal origin for supply to other businesses may need premises approval rather than simple registration.

For allergens, food businesses must provide allergen information, and PPDS foods are subject to the stricter ingredient/allergen labelling rules often referred to as Natasha’s Law.

So your system should include:

allergen matrix

recipe/spec ingredient records

allergen change control

labelled food support

due-date / open-date controls

cleaning and temperature logs

delivery traceability

incident and corrective-action records

Business setup without digital ID

This part has changed in the UK.

Companies House guidance says identity verification became a legal requirement from 18 November 2025, with a 12-month transition period for the new regime. That affects directors, PSCs, and certain Companies House filings.

So if you mean a UK limited company filing through Companies House, digital identity verification is now part of the process.

If you want to reduce friction, the practical options are:

Option 1: Sole trader first

You can start as a sole trader and register with HMRC for Self Assessment instead of incorporating immediately. HMRC’s current guidance covers registering as a sole trader, and Making Tax Digital for Income Tax starts applying from 6 April 2026 for sole traders/landlords over the relevant threshold.

Option 2: Use an accountant / authorised service provider

For incorporation/admin support, a professional intermediary may reduce the burden, though identity verification rules still apply where required.

Option 3: Start commercially before going wide

You can validate the product with:

one flagship venue

pilot sites

consultancy + software hybrid

then formalise the bigger structure once revenue is proven

So: yes, you can start the business journey in a lower-friction way, but if you want a UK limited company route, identity verification is now part of the landscape.
